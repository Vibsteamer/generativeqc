# Independent CC-F12b center and signed-pair qualification

The restricted molecular benchmark previously computed the central energy, adjoint and full Cartesian force, then four displaced energies sequentially in one GPU process. The displaced evaluations reconverge their own SCF and CC amplitudes and do not consume the central adjoint. The intact benchmark remains available for short jobs and single-device queues.

For a long qualification, run the same restricted molecular benchmark with two roles selected by the cc-role argument. The center role returns all five energy terms and the analytic force. The signed role executes only displacement SCF and forward correlation using the same evaluator as the intact benchmark. A signed job can take both step sizes or one selected cc-step. The join accepts only matching panel, geometry, SCF inputs, shell layout, source, runtime and solver configuration. It recomputes the directional projections and calls the original full qualification gate. A partial worker reports role_passed and keeps passed false. Each original independent Cartesian force gate still runs after the join.

For one hydrated fluoride complex on an L20 the T center took 5216.92 seconds and the two signed pairs took 1419.05 and 1422.45 seconds in the original serial executable. Two concurrent physical devices could reduce the measured numerical critical path from 8058.43 to 5216.92 seconds. Additional devices cannot shorten the central solve. These timings exclude queue, compilation and upload. The evidence is a decomposition of the intact benchmark. A separately measured two-worker wall time is still required for a speedup claim.

The signed stage requires device evidence for each of the ten displaced integral channels. The offline numerical join can also replay previously qualified full-job arrays that predate this per-point resource field. Physical qualification of a newly split job always requires the field. A missing or repeated sign, step or inconsistent root keeps qualification open. Revisit grouping when different molecule sizes change the balance between the central and signed evaluations.
