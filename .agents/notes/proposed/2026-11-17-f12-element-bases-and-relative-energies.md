# Element-specific orbital bases and relative-energy acceptance for F12 labels

Status: proposed for qualification

Date: 2026-11-17

The current inventory contains H, B, C, N, O, F, Na, Al, Si, P, S, Cl, Br and I. Start the uniform-basis trial with the orbital records and correlated occupied shells below. Hold each element's policy fixed across datasets, charges, spin states, atoms, fragments, complexes and displaced geometries. A cardinal study changes the cardinal for the entire trial according to the published family. The table is the triple-zeta member. A particular orbital record is used by every electronic component at that cardinal in this trial.

| Element | TZ orbital record for the main trial | Nuclear potential and occupied shells in correlation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| H | aug-cc-pVTZ-F12 | All-electron. Correlate its occupied electrons. |
| B, C, N, O, F | aug-cc-pC(F12)VTZ | All-electron. Include 1s and valence. |
| Al, Si, P, S, Cl | aug-cc-pC(F12)VTZ | All-electron. Freeze 1s, include 2s, 2p and valence. |
| Na | cc-pCVTZ-F12 | All-electron. Freeze 1s, include 2s, 2p and valence. Check diffuse sensitivity in charged Na systems. |
| Br | aug-cc-pVTZ-PP-F12 | ECP10MDF. Freeze 3s, 3p, include 3d and valence. |
| I | aug-cc-pVTZ-PP-F12 | ECP28MDF. Freeze 4s, 4p, include 4d and valence. |

All-electron nuclear potentials retain core electrons in the mean field even if a shell is frozen for the correlation calculation. Keep all retained shells in the reference SCF. Follow the core subspace continuously on a given electronic surface. For the neutral Br and I atoms each ECP leaves 25 electrons explicit, of which the trial correlates 17. Store the orbital, potential and fitting coefficients and their identities at each cardinal. Match orbital basis, Coulomb/exchange fit, correlation fit, F12 complementary basis and ECP independently. Record effective ranks and maximum angular momenta for energies and nuclear derivatives. Literature coverage is distinct from actual registry entries or qualified GPU kernels. Qualification must include the relevant open-shell paths.

Use the F12 core-valence families for the explicit light-element core, and the augmented PP-F12 family for the remaining d shell in Br/I. Compare a representative reaction or binding energy with and without outer-core correlation at identical potential, orbital basis, geometry, auxiliary sets and geminal parameter. For Br/I also check retained outer-core s,p with a suitable weighted core-valence PP reference. For the all-electron third-period elements check the effect of releasing 1s on representative differences. Report changes by shell separately from diffuse augmentation, increasing cardinal, and changing the Hamiltonian. Once the target space is selected, a total that already correlates those shells includes their effect. The earlier data used all-electron Br and a zero frozen-orbital count. Version the new protocol and recompute its atomic references before mixing any absolute-energy labels or interpreting cross-protocol comparisons.

Define a benchmark observable from the published geometries, electronic states and stoichiometric coefficients, for example a reaction energy, conformer separation, barrier or interaction energy. Score the same observable first against a higher-cardinal, matched-Hamiltonian, matched-core anchor for basis convergence and separately against the selected benchmark reference for external accuracy. Keep spin–orbit, zero-point, thermal, higher-excitation and other physical corrections explicit when comparing to literature values. Use the single-geometry energy for every term. The proposed screening target is 0.1 kcal/mol per observable with a 0.2 kcal/mol upper tolerance on the declared matched validation set. Report denominator, signed bias, MAE, maximum absolute error, each exceedance and unresolved cases by subset. A particular finite cardinal requires evidence from several chemical classes. Keep total single-point energies and independent nuclear-force checks, including reconverged energies at two displacement sizes.

The initial branch qualifications cover native components and a conventional hydrogen control. A molecular F12 energy and its nuclear gradient, a nonzero triples case and the scalar ECP/open-shell endpoint need their own numerical oracles and L20 timings. Compare globally fixed valence-plus-core-increment, hydrogen augmentation and focal-extrapolation variants as separate protocols before a production choice. Apply the same basis/cardinal and frozen-shell rules to atomic reference energies.
